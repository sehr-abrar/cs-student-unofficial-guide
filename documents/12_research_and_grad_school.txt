SHOULD YOU DO RESEARCH?

Undergrad research is valuable in two cases: (1) you're seriously considering a PhD and need to know what research is actually like, or (2) there's a problem space you're genuinely curious about that isn't covered by coursework.

Research is not valuable as a resume line if you show up, get confused, do some literature review, and leave after one semester. Professors have seen this pattern many times. A meaningful research contribution takes sustained engagement, usually over 2+ semesters.

If you're uncertain, start with a reading group or join an existing project as a contributor before committing to an official research credit.

---

HOW TO FIND A RESEARCH ADVISOR

Go to professor websites. Read their recent papers (abstracts and introductions are enough to start). Email 3–5 professors whose work you actually find interesting. In the email: explain what you found interesting about a specific paper or project, describe your relevant background, and ask if they have research opportunities for undergraduates.

Personalized emails get responses. Generic "I want to do research" emails don't.

Most professors will invite you to a short meeting before deciding. Come prepared: know their recent work, have a question ready, and know what skills you're bringing.

---

PHD APPLICATIONS

For CS PhD programs, the most important factors in roughly this order:
1. Research experience and publications (even co-author on a workshop paper matters)
2. Strong letters of recommendation from people who have seen your research ability
3. Statement of purpose that is specific about what you want to study and why
4. GRE is no longer required by most programs
5. GPA matters somewhat but a 3.5 with publications beats a 4.0 without research experience

Apply to 8–12 programs across a range of rankings. Top-5 programs are near-lottery even with strong applications. Middle-tier R1 programs have excellent faculty and more predictable admissions.

---

TIMELINE

Junior year fall: start identifying professors to email, take core courses that demonstrate aptitude.
Junior year spring: begin outreach for summer research positions (REUs, on-campus research).
Senior year fall: GRE prep (if applying to programs that require it), draft statement of purpose, get letters confirmed.
Senior year November–December: applications due (most deadlines December 1–15).
March–April: admission decisions and visit weekends.

The timeline feels far away until it doesn't. Start a year earlier than you think you need to.
