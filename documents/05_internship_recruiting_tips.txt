RECRUITING TIMELINE (THIS IS EARLIER THAN YOU THINK)

Big tech internship recruiting starts in August/September for the following summer. If you're applying for a summer internship and you wait until January, you've already missed Google, Meta, Amazon, and most large companies. Their processes are:
1. Online application (August–October)
2. Online assessment (OA) — 1–2 coding problems on HackerRank or similar
3. Phone/video interview (1–2 rounds, 45 min each)
4. Offer (November–December, sometimes later for smaller companies)

Startups and smaller companies recruit later, usually November through March.

---

WHAT ACTUALLY GETS YOU INTERVIEWS

In order of impact:
1. Referrals — a current employee submitting your resume is more valuable than almost anything else. Start building these relationships sophomore year or earlier.
2. Projects on your resume that show you can build things. Not homework projects. Real things: open source contributions, personal tools other people use, research code.
3. GPA matters for some companies (Google cares, many don't). A 3.2 is rarely the reason you don't get an interview; a blank resume is.

Leetcode: you need to practice. Easy problems fluently, medium problems reliably. Hard problems are rarely asked in first-round screens and commonly asked in on-site/final rounds at top companies.

---

WHAT TO PUT ON YOUR RESUME

One page. No objectives sections. Use action verbs ("built," "reduced," "designed") with measurable results where possible ("reduced API latency by 40%"). Your class projects are fine for your first resume — just describe them accurately. Don't claim you "led" something if you didn't.

Skills section: list the languages and tools you actually know. If someone asks you to write a merge sort in a language you listed and you can't, that's a bad interview.

---

NEGOTIATING OFFERS

You can negotiate internship offers. Most companies expect it. The scripts are simple:
"I'm very excited about this offer. I have another offer at $X/week — is there any flexibility on the compensation?"

You don't need a competing offer to negotiate, but having one helps. The worst they say is no. Nobody has ever had an internship offer rescinded for politely asking about compensation.

---

REJECTIONS

You will get rejected. Many times. Students who land top internships often have 50+ applications, 20 OAs, 10 first-rounds, 3 final rounds, and 2 offers. The funnel is brutal and mostly not about you. Apply widely, track everything in a spreadsheet, and don't treat rejections as information about your worth.
